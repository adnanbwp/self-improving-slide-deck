## RESEARCHER LENS

### R1. Empirical evidence (2025-2026) on AI coding agents' effect on team-level delivery metrics — individual speedup vs team outcomes

- **METR's original RCT (Feb-Jun 2025, 16 experienced OSS devs, 246 real tasks) found developers were 19% SLOWER with AI access, despite forecasting 24% faster and self-reporting 20% faster after the fact.** Code quality was equivalent with/without AI. This is the strongest individual-level causal (RCT) evidence available and it points to a *negative* speedup on unfamiliar, deeply-owned codebases. — [METR blog: "We are Changing our Developer Productivity Experiment Design"](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/); corroborated by [Zvi Mowshowitz's analysis](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/on-metrs-ai-coding-rct) and multiple press summaries (sciencereader.com, augmentcode.com). **Confidence: High** (RCT design, pre-registered, multiple independent write-ups agree on the 19% figure).
- **METR's follow-up (Aug 2025+, 57 devs, 800+ tasks, lower pay) is compromised by severe selection bias** — 30-50% of developers refused to submit tasks where AI's advantage looked large, and many refused to participate in AI-disallowed conditions at all. Raw follow-up numbers (-18% original cohort, -4% new cohort) likely *underestimate* true AI gains, per METR's own admission. This means the famous "19% slower" headline is time-bound and methodologically fragile — not a stable industry constant. — [METR blog](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/). **Confidence: High** on the bias existing; **Medium** on what the "true" number actually is.
- **DORA's 2025 State of AI-Assisted Software Development report (~5,000 respondents, Google/DORA) frames AI as an "amplifier"**: it magnifies whatever organizational strengths or dysfunctions already exist rather than fixing them. 90% AI adoption (up from ~76%), but throughput gains without strong version control / small-batch practices / testing discipline convert into *instability*, not net delivery improvement. — [dora.dev/dora-report-2025](https://dora.dev/dora-report-2025/); [Google Cloud Blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report); [InfoQ summary](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/03/ai-dora-report/). **Confidence: High** (large survey, official DORA channel, multiple secondary confirmations).
- **Faros AI's 2026 telemetry (22,000 developers, 1,255 teams) is the clearest team-vs-individual divergence dataset found**: individual output metrics rose sharply (tasks/dev +21-33.7%, epics/dev +66.2%, PRs merged/dev +16.2% to +98% depending on year) while **organizational delivery metrics stayed flat or degraded** — bugs/dev +54%, incidents per PR +242.7%. Faros calls this the "AI Productivity Paradox" / "Acceleration Whiplash": real gains at the top of the pipeline, compounding quality costs downstream. — [Faros AI: Key Takeaways from the DORA Report 2025](https://www.faros.ai/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-dora-report-2025). **Confidence: Medium-High** (single vendor's telemetry, but directionally consistent with DORA's own amplifier framing and with GitClear/LinearB data below — triangulated across 3 independent datasets).
- **Danluu (July 2026), a credible practitioner source (ex-Google/Microsoft, well-known benchmarking blogger), independently corroborates the "individual speedup, team/system risk" pattern from a qualitative angle**: agentic workflows can be genuinely fast, but only when paired with heavy automated testing (fuzzing) discipline; without it, "everything that's not constrained from degrading will rapidly degrade" as code volume scales. Also documents an instance of an agent fabricating a bug repro video — a concrete example of why raw throughput isn't the same as trustworthy throughput. — [danluu.com/ai-coding](https://danluu.com/ai-coding/). **Confidence: Medium** (single author, but detailed first-hand technical account, consistent with METR and DORA).

**Synthesis for R1:** Individual-level speedup is real but contested and context-dependent (negative in METR's clean RCT on unfamiliar OSS repos; positive in DX/Booking.com and vendor telemetry on familiar codebases with agent-friendly tooling). Team/organizational-level delivery metrics are consistently *not* rising in proportion to individual output — the gap shows up as review latency, incident rate, and churn, not as faster releases. This is the single most important finding for the deck: **velocity per developer and velocity per squad have decoupled.**

### R2. Formally proposed measurement frameworks for AI-assisted/agentic teams

- **DX Core 4 (GetDX)** unifies DORA + SPACE + DevEx into four dimensions: Speed, Effectiveness, Quality, Business Impact. GetDX has since layered an **"AI Measurement Framework"** on top spanning Utilization, Impact, and Cost. 2026 telemetry: 27.4% of production code is now AI-generated on average (Q1 2026); daily AI users save 4.72 hrs/week. Booking.com case study (3,500 devs): 16% productivity lift attributed to AI, 31% total throughput increase, 150,000 hours saved year one — but this is a vendor-published case study, not independently audited. — [getdx.com/dx-core-4](https://getdx.com/dx-core-4/); [GetDX AI Measurement Framework guide](https://getdx.com/blog/ai-measurement-framework-guide/); [byteiota summary](https://byteiota.com/dx-core-4-how-unified-metrics-fix-ai-productivity-gap/). **Confidence: Medium** (framework itself well-documented; Booking.com numbers are vendor-sourced, single case).
- **DORA's AI Capabilities Model (2025, official DORA/Google publication)** names 7 capabilities that determine whether AI amplifies good or bad outcomes: clear/communicated AI stance, healthy data ecosystems, AI-accessible internal data, strong version control, working in small batches, user-centric focus, quality internal platforms. Notably this is a *capabilities* model, not a metrics model — DORA explicitly recommends organizations audit these conditions rather than chase a new AI-era velocity number. — [dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model/report](https://dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model/report/); [Google Cloud Blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-doras-inaugural-ai-capabilities-model). **Confidence: High** (primary source, official DORA publication).
- **Scrum.org: "From Velocity to Agent Efficiency: Evidence-Based Management for the AI Era"** argues Velocity becomes a "vanity metric" once an agent can generate a 3-point story's code in 4 seconds — the constraint moves to verification, not drafting. Recommends: (a) retire Velocity, (b) use Evidence-Based Management (EBM) — Scrum.org's existing outcome-based framework (measures value-delivery ability under uncertainty, not output) — as the substitute lens, (c) stop assigning story points to agent work; instead track **agent capacity via token budgets/compute cost**, and (d) shift "Activity Metrics" to "Outcome Metrics." A companion Scrum.org post, "Beyond Velocity Metrics: How to Measure AI-Agile Synchronization," extends this into measuring the friction between the "silicon workforce" and "carbon workforce." — [scrum.org: Velocity to Agent Efficiency](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/velocity-agent-efficiency-evidence-based-management-ai-era); [scrum.org: Beyond Velocity Metrics](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/beyond-velocity-metrics-how-measure-ai-agile-synchronization). **Confidence: Medium** (single-org source, but Scrum.org is the closest thing to an official Scrum standards body, and the recommendation is explicit and directly on-topic). Note: WebFetch could not retrieve the article body directly (JS-rendered page); findings sourced from search-engine summary of the same URL — flag for manual verification before quoting verbatim in the deck.
- **SPACE framework retains currency but is explicitly being reframed for agentic contexts**: multiple 2026 practitioner sources (LinearB, Axify, DZone, Uplevel) converge on the same argument — Activity alone is now misleading (an agent can inflate Activity metrics trivially), so Satisfaction, Communication/Collaboration, and Efficiency/Flow become the load-bearing dimensions, cross-checked against delivery outcomes. A FORGE '26 (IEEE/ACM) peer-reviewed paper, *"Impacts of Generative AI on Agile Teams' Productivity: A Multi-Case Longitudinal Study,"* explicitly uses SPACE as its analytical lens to study GenAI's effect on velocity, story points, and estimation accuracy across multiple real Agile teams — this is the most academically rigorous R2/R3 crossover source found. — [arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13766) (FORGE '26); [DZone: SPACE Framework in the AI Era](https://dzone.com/articles/space-framework-ai-developer-productivity). **Confidence: Medium** (peer-reviewed venue is a strong signal; full findings were not extractable from the PDF fetch — only methodology confirmed, not the numeric results. Flagged for a follow-up direct read of the paper).

### R3. Estimation accuracy / story point validity when AI does implementation; code durability/rework data

- **GitClear's 2026 "Maintainability Gap" report (623M code changes analyzed, 2023-2026)** is the most rigorous, largest-sample evidence on AI-era code durability found. Key numbers: refactoring activity collapsed from 21% (2022) to 3.8% of changed lines; block/code duplication up 81%; copy-paste within commits up 41%; error-masking constructs up 47%; two-week code churn up 15%; developers now ~5x more likely to duplicate than refactor vs. pre-AI baseline. This directly answers "code durability/rework" — durability is declining, not improving, as AI writes more of the code. — [gitclear.com/the_ai_code_quality_maintainability_gap](https://www.gitclear.com/the_ai_code_quality_maintainability_gap). The 2025 predecessor report (211M lines, Google/Microsoft/Meta + enterprise repos) found copy-paste up 8.3%→12.3% (2020-24) and churn up 3.1%→5.7% — same direction, independent earlier sample. — [gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research); corroborated by [jonas.rs summary](https://www.jonas.rs/2025/02/09/report-summary-gitclear-ai-code-quality-research-2025.html) and [Rob Bowley's blog](https://blog.robbowley.net/2025/02/17/gitclears-latest-report-indicates-genai-is-having-a-negative-impact-on-code-quality/). **Confidence: High** (two independent GitClear vintages, large samples, external corroboration; GitClear is a single commercial vendor so treat exact percentages as directionally reliable rather than precise).
- **No direct, rigorous study was found that measures "story point validity" (i.e., whether a 3-point story still costs 3x a 1-point story once AI implements it) empirically at scale.** The closest is the FORGE '26 peer-reviewed paper (see R2) which uses SPACE to study velocity/story-point/estimation-accuracy shifts in real teams — but the fetched excerpt only confirmed the study *asks* this question; the numeric answer was not retrievable via the tool used. **Mark as partial SOURCE GAP** — direction (frameworks exist, are being studied) is confirmed; magnitude is not. **Confidence: Low** on any specific estimation-accuracy number; **Medium** that the research question is actively and rigorously being investigated.
- Practitioner commentary (Medium/InfoQ, non-peer-reviewed) is loud that "story points are outdated guesses" once agents draft code in seconds, but this is opinion/argument, not measurement — kept separate from evidence above and cited under E2/incentives instead. — [Medium: "Story Points Are Dying"](https://medium.com/@amitkalghatgi/story-points-are-dying-welcome-to-the-era-of-prompt-points-75d31e44bf9c). **Confidence: Low** (single-author opinion piece).

### R4. Evidence on human review capacity as the new bottleneck

- **DORA/Faros telemetry: median PR review time up 441% year-over-year (2026 dataset) vs. 91% in the 2025 dataset**, across 22,000 developers / 1,255 teams. PR size up 51.3% (2026) after +154% (2025). 31% more PRs merge with zero human review. — [Faros AI: Key Takeaways from DORA Report 2025](https://www.faros.ai/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-dora-report-2025). **Confidence: High** for the direction and rough magnitude (large sample, cited across multiple secondary write-ups); **Medium** on the exact "441%" figure since it originates from one vendor's proprietary telemetry rather than a peer-reviewed instrument.
- **CircleCI 2026 + LinearB 2026 Software Engineering Benchmarks (independent data providers) triangulate the same trend**: feature-branch throughput up 59% YoY while main-branch success rate fell to 70.8%; agentic-AI PRs have 5.3x longer pickup time than unassisted PRs; AI-assisted PRs wait 2.47x longer overall, and in the most cited figure, AI-generated PRs wait **4.6x longer before a reviewer even opens them**. Stack Overflow 2025 survey: trust in AI code accuracy has fallen to 29%. — [Codacy blog synthesizing CircleCI/LinearB/Faros/Stack Overflow data](https://blog.codacy.com/ai-breaking-code-review-how-engineering-teams-survive-pr-bottleneck) (vault copy: `botsitting`/`ai-breaking-code-review` articles). **Confidence: High** — three independent commercial telemetry sources (CircleCI, LinearB, Faros) and one independent survey (Stack Overflow) all point the same direction; this is about as triangulated as vendor telemetry gets.
- **Quality signal backing the "reviewers are right to be slow" reading**: CodeRabbit study found AI-written code surfaces 1.7x more issues than human-written code; 96% of developers in the 2026 State of Code Developer Survey don't fully trust AI-generated code's functional accuracy. — [dev.to: "Code Review Is the Real Bottleneck of 2026"](https://dev.to/code-board/code-review-is-the-real-bottleneck-of-2026-and-most-teams-dont-see-it-5eed). **Confidence: Medium** (secondary aggregation article; underlying CodeRabbit/State-of-Code primary reports not independently verified in this pass — flag for follow-up).
- **Vault corroboration (qualitative, HN discussion, 15 comments)**: practitioners independently describe the same bottleneck and are already improvising fixes — pushing deterministic checks (linters, secret scanning, e2e tests) ahead of human review, reserving humans for "intent and design," and experimenting with AI-generated risk scores to decide which PRs need a human at all. One HN commenter explicitly proposes WIP-limit-style dynamic thresholds: risk score above a team's comfort level routes to a human, everything else auto-approves. — [HN: "Ask HN: How is your org managing PR review load as AI multiplies code output?"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48329446). **Confidence: Low-Medium** (anecdotal, small thread, but directly relevant as a source of real practitioner cadence redesign ideas — useful for the deck's "what replaces review" section even though it's not measurement-grade evidence).

## ECONOMIST LENS

### E1. Real unit economics: cost-per-task, token spend at team level, cost-blowout predictions

- **Per-task cost comparisons (multiple 2026 vendor/analyst sources, triangulated directionally)**: AI coding agents run roughly $2-8 per task vs. $50-200/hour for a human developer; more granular figures put a bug fix at ~$0.54 and a feature-sized task at ~$2.28 on Claude Sonnet 4.6. These are useful for order-of-magnitude framing but come from vendor/consultancy blogs, not audited enterprise data — treat as illustrative, not precise. — [search-aggregated from morphllm.com, cowork.ink, iternal.ai]. **Confidence: Low-Medium** (directionally consistent across sources, but no single authoritative primary source; figures vary by an order of magnitude depending on task complexity and model).
- **Team/enterprise-level token spend is rising fast and outpacing per-token price drops**: average enterprise AI spend went from ~$1.2M (2024) to ~$7M (2026) even as per-token API prices fell ~280x over the same period — because agentic systems consume 5-30x more tokens per task than a chat interaction (5,000-15,000 tokens for simple tool-calling agents; 200,000-1,000,000+ for complex multi-agent systems). Anthropic's own published enterprise figures: ~$13/developer/active-day, $150-250/developer/month typical on Claude Code, with heavy-automation shops reaching $500-2,000/engineer/month and outlier cases hitting $20,000/month. — [CIO.com: "AI coding token costs are on track to rival human payroll"](https://www.cio.com/article/4189149/ai-coding-token-costs-are-on-track-to-rival-human-payroll.html); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/06/24/ai-coding-agents-could-soon-cost-more-than-the-developers-using-them/5260864); [Fortune on Microsoft's cost disclosures](https://fortune.com/2026/05/22/microsoft-ai-cost-problem-tokens-agents/). **Confidence: Medium-High** (multiple independent outlets — CIO, The Register, Fortune — converging on the same "token costs rivaling payroll" narrative, and Anthropic's own disclosed figures are a primary source for the low end of the range).
- **Gartner: over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027**, citing escalating costs, unclear business value, and inadequate risk controls; a related framing cited elsewhere puts data-preparation-driven cost overruns in the 60-75% range for projects that treat data prep as an afterthought. Gartner also separately predicts $234B of enterprise application software spend is "at risk" from agentic AI by an unspecified date. — [Gartner press release (June 2025, cancellations by 2027)](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027) — note: direct WebFetch of gartner.com returned HTTP 403 (bot-blocked); content triangulated via [HPCwire syndication](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/this-just-in/gartner-predicts-over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027/) and [AgentMarketCap](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/12/gartner-40-percent-agentic-ai-project-cancellation-2027), both of which quote the same Gartner press release verbatim; [Gartner $234B press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-01-gartner-says-us-dollars-234-billion-in-enterprise-application-software-spend-is-at-risk-from-agentic-artificial-intelligence) (title/date confirmed via search, body not independently fetched). **Confidence: Medium-High** on the 40%/2027 figure (original Gartner press release exists and is corroborated by two independent syndications quoting it identically); **Low** on the 60-75% cost-overrun figure (single secondary mention, original Gartner sourcing not confirmed — flag as unverified).

### E2. Incentives: who profits from keeping story points/velocity vs. who profits from declaring agile dead

- **Explicit "agile is dead" commentary is traceable to specific named actors with a commercial stake in the replacement narrative**: Steve Jones, EVP at Capgemini, publicly declared "AI has killed the Agile Manifesto" — a claim that directly positions Capgemini's AI-native delivery consulting services as the successor. McKinsey published a YouTube video titled "Moving away from Agile: What's Next," similarly positioning its own advisory offer. AWS's 2026 prescriptive guidance proposes replacing "Sprint Planning" with "Intent Design" — a reframe that happens to align with selling AWS's agentic tooling stack. — [InfoQ: "Does AI Make the Agile Manifesto Obsolete?"](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/ai-agile-manifesto-debate/); [Age-of-Product.com: "Agile Is Dead, Long Live Agility"](https://age-of-product.com/agile-is-dead-long-live-agility/) (explicitly names the consultant/vendor incentive pattern: "consulting narratives have a way of 'ending' whatever you're doing to make room for the next wave of services"). **Confidence: Medium-High** (named individuals and firms, explicit quotes, cross-referenced by InfoQ's independent reporting of the same debate).
- **Counter-evidence that "agile is dead" is itself contested/overstated**: Forrester's 2025 State of Agile Development report has 95% of respondents still affirming Agile's relevance — a data point that itself has an incentive structure worth flagging (Forrester sells agile-adjacent research/advisory too), so triangulate rather than take either side as neutral. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/ai-agile-manifesto-debate/); [Medium: "Agile 2026: AI Did Not Make Agile Obsolete"](https://medium.com/agile-digital-transformation-coaching/agile-2026-ai-did-not-make-agile-obsolete-but-heres-what-really-happened-a2982ebdd70c). **Confidence: Medium** (single survey source cited across secondary pieces, original Forrester report not independently pulled).
- **Story points/velocity side of the incentive question is documented but softer**: story points are taught in every major Scrum certification course despite never appearing in the Scrum Guide itself — a structural incentive for certification bodies (Scrum.org, Scrum Alliance) to keep teaching a practice that isn't formally prescribed. Separately, well-documented critiques (LinearB, GetDX, Serious Scrum/Medium) show velocity tracking creates gaming incentives (estimate inflation, discouraged collaboration) independent of any AI angle — this is a pre-existing critique that the AI moment has simply made louder, not a new phenomenon. — [Scrum Alliance: "The Story Point Controversy"](https://resources.scrumalliance.org/Article/story-point-controversy); [GetDX: "Beyond story points"](https://getdx.com/blog/developer-velocity/). **Confidence: Medium** (well-established, longstanding critique; the "certification bodies profit from teaching it" framing is Pax's inference from the sourced facts, not a direct quote from any source — flag this inference explicitly if used in the deck).

### E3. If velocity becomes near-free, what becomes scarce (judgement, spec quality, review attention)

- **The "judgment bottleneck" / "taste is the new bottleneck" argument is a live, multi-author 2026 essay genre, not a single source** — convergent independent essays (Jon C. Phillips's "Judgment Bottleneck," designative.info's "Taste Is the New Bottleneck," multiple Medium/IT Chronicles pieces) all make the same structural argument: once code generation is ~free, the scarce, non-scalable resource is knowing *what* to build and *whether* the output is actually good — framing, intent-setting, and evaluation. — [joncphillips.com/judgment-bottleneck](https://joncphillips.com/judgment-bottleneck/); [designative.info: "Taste Is the New Bottleneck"](https://www.designative.info/2026/02/01/taste-is-the-new-bottleneck-design-strategy-and-judgment-in-the-age-of-agents-and-vibe-coding/); [Medium/IT Chronicles: "The Judgment Bottleneck"](https://medium.com/@isacco.sordo/the-judgment-bottleneck-software-engineering-in-the-age-of-ai-f0fd5cffb57e). **Confidence: Medium** (opinion-essay genre rather than empirical research, but the convergence across independent authors is itself a signal worth citing, and it aligns directionally with the DORA "amplifier" finding and the vault's own "80% Problem" concept below).
- **Kent Beck (Agile Manifesto co-author) makes the same argument from inside the agile tradition, which strengthens it**: his "augmented coding" framing explicitly says the skills that matter shift from language/syntax expertise to "vision, strategy, task breakdown, and feedback loops" — i.e., judgment and specification quality become the differentiator once implementation is delegated. — [Pragmatic Engineer: "TDD, AI agents and coding with Kent Beck"](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/tdd-ai-agents-and-coding-with-kent); [Heavybit/O11ycast: "Augmented Coding with Kent Beck"](https://www.heavybit.com/library/podcasts/o11ycast/ep-80-augmented-coding-with-kent-beck). **Confidence: Medium-High** (named, credible primary voice with direct relevant authority — one of the four original Agile Manifesto authors — though this is his stated opinion, not a controlled study).
- **Vault corroboration**: the myPKA vault's own "80% Problem in Agentic Coding" note (sourced from Addy Osmani via an internal course) makes the identical argument independently — AI handles the ~80% that's well-specified; the remaining 20% (edge cases, ambiguity resolution, architectural trade-offs) is where human judgment concentrates, and it cites the METR 19%-longer finding as the mechanism (time shifts from writing into verifying). — `/mnt/c/Users/adnan/Google Drive/aa2brain/concepts/the-80-percent-problem.md` (internal note, sourced from `raw/courses/new-sdlc-with-vibe-coding-2026-06-28.md`). **Confidence: Medium** (internal synthesis note, but independently arrives at the same conclusion as the external essay genre and ties it to the same primary METR data point — genuine triangulation, not circular citation).

### E4. "Sustainable pace" cost-wise when the constraint is human attention — published cognitive-load/attention-budgeting frameworks

- **Glean's Work AI Institute "Work AI Index" (survey of 6,000 digital workers, co-authored with researchers from Stanford, UC Berkeley, Emory, and others) is the strongest empirical source found for this question.** It quantifies the "botsitting" tax directly: AI saves ~11 hours/week but workers lose ~6.4 of those hours back to supervision (context-feeding, quality-checking, debugging, tool-switching) — net savings eroded by ~58%. Only 13% report AI significantly improved company performance despite 87% adoption. It also names the failure mode of ignoring this constraint: "botshitting" — 69% admit shipping unverified AI work; 41% sometimes can't explain work they deliver. — [CIO.com: "The Hidden Cost of Enterprise AI: 6.4 Hours a Week Babysitting Bots"](https://www.cio.com/article/4183804/the-hidden-cost-of-enterprise-ai-6-4-hours-a-week-babysitting-bots.html) (vault copy: `raw/articles/botsitting-hidden-cost-enterprise-ai-cio.md`, synthesized in `concepts/botsitting-tax.md`). **Confidence: High** (large multi-university-backed survey, specific named co-authors, consistent internal vault synthesis).
- **No formal, named "attention-budgeting" framework equivalent to a token budget (i.e., a codified per-person hours/week cap for AI supervision, analogous to WIP limits) was found published by a credible source.** The closest analogues are (a) the Glean "botsitting hours" measurement above, which quantifies the cost but doesn't prescribe a budget/limit, and (b) academic framing of "sphere of accountability" expansion — the argument that AI expands what a person is responsible for monitoring without expanding their time, which is conceptually the "sustainable pace" argument applied to attention rather than hours worked. — [search-aggregated academic commentary, 2026, exact primary paper not confirmed in this pass]. **Mark as SOURCE GAP**: an explicit "attention budget" framework, named and formalized, does not appear to exist yet in the literature — this is itself a finding worth stating plainly to Larry: **the deck may be proposing something genuinely novel here, not summarizing an existing framework.** **Confidence: Low** on there being an existing named framework; **Medium-High** on the underlying cost data (Glean) that would ground a new one.
- **Scrum.org's "silicon workforce vs carbon workforce" framing (see R2) is the closest thing to a named practitioner concept for team-level capacity planning under agents**, explicitly recommending token/compute budgets for agent "capacity" as a parallel structure to human capacity — implying (but not fully specifying) that human review/oversight capacity needs its own explicit budget line in sprint planning. — [scrum.org: Velocity to Agent Efficiency](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/velocity-agent-efficiency-evidence-based-management-ai-era). **Confidence: Low-Medium** (directionally relevant, not a fully worked-out framework, single-org source).

## Methodology note
