# Persuasion Scorecard — hybrid-delivery v3.0.0 (canonical)

**Boundary note:** Aria authored the narrative spine (sanctioned SHAPE exception); the deck is Coda's execution. This scorecard evaluates the execution.

## Audience model (updated for the v3.0.0 objective)

- **Priors:** IMs/DMs prove their worth through delivery numbers — velocity, predictability, flow. Their engineers are already ahead of them on agent adoption. Most have watched "AI features" get bolted onto Jira and ceremonies without anything changing.
- **Values:** team outcomes over individual heroics; honest reporting; being useful in the room rather than titled. Distribution within the segment: a pragmatic majority who want Monday-morning moves, an analytical ~15% who will pull the sources, and a defensive minority whose professional identity is *the velocity report itself*.
- **Anxieties:** being bypassed by the tech lead; being told their metrics are theatre; being handed governance work framed as a land-grab they can't win; "AI transformation" arriving as a mandate from above.
- **JTBD:** walk out with (a) a defensible answer to "what do we measure now?", (b) a first move that needs no permission, (c) language to use with stakeholders and the tech lead.
- **Current belief state (Adnan's brief, verbatim):** the organisation "has only questions at the moment and no answers." The audience is *aware the ground moved* but has no proposed successor practice. Activation gap: small for the audit (they already sensed the coordination pain); moderate for the measurement thesis (it dethrones their own number); largest for piloting an unproven framework — which the deck correctly makes an opt-in invitation, not homework.

## What moved the score

**Audience fit 8.5 → 9.0 — the deck now argues on the IM's own turf.** v2.1.0 opened on PR review time (an engineering metric) and asked the IM to *take* governance work. v3.0.0 opens on velocity decoupling (the IM's own number), and slide 12 draws the sensors-vs-decisions line: engineering reads the signals, the IM owns the decisions the signals force. That converts "grab the governance work" into "you already own the delivery outcome — this is what owning it now means." It is the single biggest persuasion upgrade in the deck's history because it dissolves the contest the audience most fears losing.

**Emotional resonance 7.5 → 8.0 — the crisis is now personal.** "The number you use to prove delivery just stopped meaning anything" lands on identity, not tooling. The mitigation is built in: the Jeffries pre-concession ("velocity was already soft — agents just made gaming it free") tells the audience the flaw was never their failure, which lets them accept the dethroning without defending it. The fork (15) and the first-person beat (06) carry forward.

**Respect signal 9.0 → 9.5 — the honesty is structural now.** Proposals labeled as proposals on the slide face ("Open territory; my proposal," "No published framework budgets attention yet"), caveats welded to their claims (441% "one triangulating signal"; "+40% — for complex, generative work"), a derived figure shown as derived (58% = 6.4÷11), and a full checkable source list (appendix 41). For a skeptical practitioner room this is the deck's strongest credibility posture to date — it claims exactly as much as it can ground, and the "leading voice" positioning comes from that restraint.

## What's traded or at risk

**CTA strength held at 7.5 — a wash that should have been a gain.** The action set improved: the audit is now paired with a one-sprint honest-metric baseline (cycle-time of judged outcomes, review-queue depth, or 30-day survival — all concrete), and the framework pilot is correctly an opt-in invitation, not an assignment. But the ownership-activating close that earned the v1.5.0 CTA its +1.5 — "The capability gap closes on its own. The governance gap is yours to close." — fell off the slide face into the notes, and the CTA says "five-question audit" while slide 21 lists six. Both are flagged as the narrative scorecard's one FAIL. **With those two copy fixes applied, CTA rises to 8.5 and overall to ~8.5.**

**Cognitive load in the climb (16–19).** Four dense framework slides in a row late in the session. The narrative beats are differentiated and the subordination framing on 18 works, but the pragmatic majority will absorb at most one instrument in the room — the notes' pilot-invitation posture is the right relief valve.

**Rhetorical balance:** right for this segment — logos-heavy front (checkable anomaly), one ethos beat placed early (06), pathos reserved for the fork and the close. No change recommended.

## Friction audit (audience-internal reactions to pre-empt)

1. **"So my velocity reports were theatre?"** (defensive minority) — pre-empted by Jeffries/Goodhart framing: the flaw predates agents and wasn't yours; what changed is the cost of gaming. Presenter should say "this was never your failure" out loud at slide 08.
2. **"You're asking me to bet my squad on something nobody uses."** — pre-empted by the CTA's structure (baseline = safe homework; pilot = invitation) and the "no published framework" honesty. Keep the pilot voluntary in the room.
3. **"Is the token budget a finance pitch?"** — slide 18's "constraint, not a score; under-spend isn't a virtue" line is the answer; the presenter should lead with it if the room bristles at dollar talk.
4. **"My tech lead will say this is theirs."** — slide 12 is the prepared answer; the presenter should name the split explicitly: *keep* the dashboards, *own* the decisions.

## CTA clarity score: 7.5 / 10
Specific, staged, permission-free, and calibrated (audit → baseline → opt-in pilot). Held below 8.5 only by the missing on-face ownership close and the five/six-question mismatch — both two-line copy fixes on slide 23.

## Overall persuasion score: 8.3 / 10
The measurement thread converts the deck's weakest persuasion flank (the governance land-grab) into its strongest (delivery ownership entailment), and the proposal honesty gives Adnan the leading-voice posture the brief asked for. The score rises to ~8.5 on the slide-23 copy fixes; beyond that, the ceiling is delivery-side (the room's live handling of frictions 1 and 4), not deck-side.

**Most critical framing adjustment:** restore "The capability gap closes on its own. The governance gap is yours to close." to the slide-23 face — it is the one sentence that turns the close from homework into ownership, and it is currently spoken but not shown.
