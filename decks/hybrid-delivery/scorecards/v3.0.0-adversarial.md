## Steelman of the PoV (v3.0.0 form)

Individual output and delivered value have decoupled — output telemetry climbing while incidents, bugs, and review queues degrade — and velocity, which was already a soft number before agents (Jeffries, 2012), became freely gameable the moment an agent could produce points at near-zero cost. The honest unit of squad success is therefore the flow of judged, durable outcomes through the human-judgment constraint. Reading the health signals of that constraint (incidents/PR, churn, durability, review latency) is engineering's job and lives on engineering's dashboards — but the *decisions those signals force* (what counts as done, planned review capacity, where WIP is capped, what the sprint commits to, what stakeholders are told) belong to whoever owns the delivery outcome, and capping flow to a moving constraint has always been delivery-management craft. The IM who occupies that decision layer — starting with a five-question audit and one honest metric tracked beside velocity for a sprint, optionally piloting an explicitly-proposed attention budget — evolves the role; the one who waits gets bypassed.

## Motivated opponent

Unchanged in identity, changed in footing: the staff tech lead who owns the agent tooling and the Faros/GitClear/DORA dashboards. In every prior version this opponent attacked an assertion ("governance is yours" — says who?). In v3.0.0 they must attack a syllogism whose first premise concedes their strongest card. Their position is weaker; it is not gone (see A2-residual).

## Adjudication — pre-mortem attacks vs the built HTML

### C1 (Critical) — "the re-rooting walks into the tech lead's dashboard" → **RESOLVED as Critical; downgraded to A2-RESIDUAL (Significant)**

The central adjudication. Slide 12 as built:

- **The concession is on the face.** Left half: "Engineering reads the signals" — incidents/PR, churn, durability, review latency — with the note "Signals live on the tech lead's dashboards · Faros AI · GitClear." The deck no longer claims the metrics; it hands them over explicitly. This is the move that disarms the commissioned comeback.
- **The claim is the decision layer.** Right half: what counts as *done*, planned review capacity, where WIP is capped, what the sprint commits to — with stakeholder reporting carried on the annot.
- **The warrant is printed, not implied:** "Whoever owns the delivery outcome owns these calls — down to what stakeholders are told — and capping flow to a moving constraint has always been delivery craft, not tooling."
- The banned sentence ("the tech lead does not contest this") does not appear anywhere in the deck. Verified.

**Run the comeback:** *"Delivery metrics are DORA metrics — we own those dashboards too."* The built slide pre-agrees: yes, you own the dashboards; the deck claims none of them. The tech lead must now contest the warrant itself — argue that the delivery-outcome owner does *not* own done/capacity/WIP/commitment calls — which forces them to claim delivery accountability they do not organizationally hold, or to argue those calls belong to no one, which is the deck's fork slide (15: one accountable owner beats handoffs). The syllogism is earned: (1) sensors ≠ decisions, (2) the delivery-outcome owner owns the decisions, (3) the IM holds delivery-outcome accountability. The trap is well-constructed.

**What survives (A2-RESIDUAL, Significant):** the tech lead's best remaining move is to contest premise 2's *exclusivity*: "the DoD, WIP caps, and sprint commitments are whole-team artifacts I shape as much as you do — you've shown the decision layer exists, not that the IM wins it." The deck's answers (the warrant; one-accountable-owner at 15; flow-craft fluency at 12/17) make this a fight the IM can now win in the room — but the deck still carries **no direct evidence** that organizations in practice allocate the decision layer to the IM rather than letting it drift to the tech lead (the same de facto drift the deck itself warns about on slide 10). This is an evidence gap, not a logic gap — the difference between v3.0.0 and every prior version. Significant, not Critical, because the deck now openly frames occupation as the *call to action* rather than a settled fact, which is internally consistent with not having the evidence.

**Required resolution (next cycle):** one grounded example — a named org, case study, or survey signal — of a delivery manager holding the done/capacity/commitment calls in an agentic squad would close A2 outright. Until then it caps the score below 8.5.
